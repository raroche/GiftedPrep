# Florida gifted identification

## The state rule

Eligibility is set by **State Board of Education Rule 6A-6.03019, F.A.C.**,
"Special Instructional Programs for Students who are Gifted". A student is
eligible under **Plan A** when all three hold:

1. **Superior intellectual development** — a score **two standard deviations or
   more above the mean** on an *individually administered* test of intellectual
   functioning. On a WISC-V (SD 15) that is **130**.
2. **A majority of characteristics** of gifted students, evidenced on a
   district checklist covering learning, motivation, creativity and leadership.
3. **Evidence of need** for a special instructional program.

Districts may also adopt **Plan B**, formally a *District Plan for Increasing
Participation of Underrepresented Students*, filed as **Appendix C** of the
district's ESE Policies and Procedures (SP&P) document and audited by FLDOE.
Plan B typically lowers the intellectual bar to **one standard deviation above
the mean, meaning about 115**, for students who are **low income** and/or
**English learners**.

There is no FLDOE-published list of which districts have an approved Plan B. The
authoritative way to check any district is the public SP&P repository:
<https://beessgsw.org/#/spp/institution/public/> — look at Part III / Appendix C
for the district in question.

## The screening pipeline

    referral (parent, teacher, student, MTSS, or a data sweep)
      -> group screening test + characteristics checklist
      -> parental consent
      -> individual IQ evaluation by a school psychologist
      -> eligibility staffing
      -> Educational Plan (EP)

**The group test is only a gate.** It is not the test that decides eligibility.
That is why familiarization with the group screener is worth so little, and why
a child who screens out one year can be referred again.

## Which group screener a district uses varies — a lot

Two districts verified in detail from their FLDOE-accepted 2025-26 SP&P filings
(both audited "Accepted" on 2025-10-23):

| | **Collier County** | **Monroe County** |
|---|---|---|
| Group screener | **CogAT** (form/edition not published) | **Raven's 2**, 20 minutes, nonverbal |
| Universal grade | "a selected grade level" — grade 2 implied, never stated | **Grade 2**, district-wide, explicit |
| Screening cut | **not published** | Raven's **110 / 75th percentile**; plus a matrix requiring **12 of 15 points** |
| IQ tests used | WISC-V, DAS-II, RIAS-2, SB-5, WNV, KABC-2 | WISC-V, RIAS-2, DAS-2, WNV, WPPSI-IV |
| Plan B | **Yes.** Three conditions: IQ **115+**, majority of characteristics, evidence of need. **No point matrix.** | **Yes.** Point matrix, IQ **115+** worth 5 points, minimum total required |
| Plan B fast track | none | Raven's **120+** plus all other criteria = immediate consideration |
| Evaluation timeline | **90 days** in attendance after consent | 60 calendar days (the general ESE rule) |
| Re-referral limit | not published | not published |

Other instruments Florida districts are known to use for screening include the
**NNAT2/NNAT3**, **OLSAT-8**, **KBIT-2**, and **Otis-Lennon**. Collier is the
only one of the two verified districts that uses CogAT.

**Practical consequence for a parent:** unless your district tells you the
instrument, you cannot know which one your child will see. Covering CogAT, NNAT
and OLSAT covers the common group screeners.

## What districts say about preparing

Collier publishes nothing at all on test preparation. Monroe does, in its parent
brochure, and it is the only such statement either district makes:

> "You cannot study for the test. An evaluation of intellectual functioning taps
> a broad range of your child's thinking, reasoning, and problem solving
> abilities. This test is similar to a 'Brain Teaser'. The manufacturer protects
> actual test content and it would be unethical for an evaluator to reveal test
> materials ahead of time. When the time comes for his or her evaluation, tell
> your child to: Relax! Listen carefully to the questions. Do his or her best.
> Have fun!"

That statement is about the **individual IQ evaluation**, and it is correct:
nobody should have those items in advance, and this app contains none. It does
not address familiarization with a **group screener's format**, which the CogAT
publisher itself recommends schools provide to every child (see
[`parent-science.md`](parent-science.md)).

## Which districts use which screener

**All twenty of the largest Florida districts are now documented**, from their
own FLDOE filings, in [`florida-districts.md`](florida-districts.md). The
headlines:

- **The NNAT is the dominant instrument.** Nine of the twenty use a Naglieri
  test as their primary group screener. Lee has already moved to the newer
  **NGAT**, and Miami-Dade names the **NGAT-NV** alongside the NNAT3, so the
  Naglieri General Ability Test appears to be replacing the NNAT3 in Florida.
- **Grade 2 is the screening year** in eleven districts. Miami-Dade screens in
  **grade 1**; Pinellas screens grade 1 but only for targeted students.
- **Cut scores vary by a full standard deviation**, from Duval's **107** to
  Manatee's **122**. A child referred for evaluation in Duval or Broward would
  be turned away in Manatee or Palm Beach. These are comparable standard scores,
  so that is a real difference in access, not a scale artifact.
- **Five districts publish no group screener at all.** Hillsborough screens from
  existing data by design, Marion uses a teacher rating scale, Manatee uses
  individually administered brief IQ screeners, Sarasota screens only on
  referral, and Pasco publishes nothing.
- **Plan B is not universal.** Pasco explicitly declines to operate one, so
  Pasco families have no reduced-IQ pathway at all.
- **Plan B thresholds cluster at 115**, but the outliers matter: Broward and
  Palm Beach set **112**; Marion sets **116** and forbids confidence intervals;
  Sarasota requires **120**; Manatee requires about **122**.
- **The fine print decides borderline cases.** Duval permits the standard error
  of measurement, Brevard explicitly forbids it, Marion allows it for Plan A but
  not Plan B. For a child scoring 113 or 114 that clause is the whole decision.

### The four districts that limit how often a child may be tested

This is the most practically important finding for a parent, and it is the only
kind of rule any district publishes about testing:

| District | Rule |
|---|---|
| **St. Johns** | CogAT and KBIT-2R may each be taken **only once in a K-12 career** |
| **Sarasota** | Same instrument not within 12 months; more than three screenings across a school career discouraged |
| **Manatee** | Screening with the RIST bars the RIAS for eligibility; screening with the WASI-II bars all Wechsler scales |
| **Osceola** | Referral for evaluation at most once per year |

**No district in the set publishes any statement about test preparation.** None
endorses it, discourages it or prohibits it. The attempt limits above are the
only published constraints, and they work by limiting tries rather than by
addressing preparation at all.

## Caveats recorded honestly

- Collier's universal screening grade is an **inference** from the phrase "for
  students outside of second grade". It is never stated.
- Collier's Appendix C attachments are internally headed "2023-2024" and were
  carried forward unchanged into the accepted 2025-26 filing. Current, but aged.
- Monroe's Plan B matrix states "10 out of 13 possible points" while its listed
  item maxima sum to 19. The inconsistency is present in both the 6/2024 and
  7/2024 revisions and in the FLDOE-accepted filing.
- Neither district publishes any re-referral or re-screening waiting period.
- **Verify your own district.** Nothing above should be assumed to apply outside
  Collier and Monroe. Contact your school's gifted or ESE coordinator, or read
  your district's Appendix C in the SP&P repository linked above.
