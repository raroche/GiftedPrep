# Part A — NNAT

## Edition status

| Edition | Levels | Status |
|---|---|---|
| NNAT2 (2007) | A-G | Superseded. Source of the four named item types. |
| **NNAT3 (2016)** | A-D | Current. **All-new items**, two parallel forms per level. |
| **NNAT3 (2016)** | E-G | Current, but a **renorming of NNAT2** — the items are identical, only the norms are new. |

## The four item types

The names come from **NNAT2**. Neither NNAT3 manual uses them; NNAT3 describes
items structurally instead. Pearson's canonical NNAT2 wording:

1. **Pattern Completion** — perceive a pattern within a large rectangle in which
   a piece is missing, and extend the pattern into the missing space.
2. **Reasoning by Analogy** — reason about the logical relationship between
   geometric shapes changing in one or more dimensions, across rows and down
   columns.
3. **Serial Reasoning** — recognise a sequence of shapes and how the sequence
   changes across rows and down columns.
4. **Spatial Visualization** — recognise how two or more designs would look if
   combined; may involve rotations or intersections.

## Universal item mechanics

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Answer options | **Exactly 5**, numbered 1-5, at every level |
| Missing cell | `?` inside a **dashed** border |
| Palette | **black, white, yellow, blue, green** only |
| Colour-blind validated | monochromacy, protanopia/deuteranopia, tritanopia |
| Verbal content | **none**; directions are pictorial |
| Item order | approximate order of difficulty |

The palette restriction is a hard design constraint, and the answer must never
be carried by hue alone.

## Administration and scoring

- **48 items, 30 minutes** of working time, at every level. 35-45 min total.
- A **power test**, not a speed test. Pearson found extra time did not raise scores.
- Group size: K up to 5 children, grade 1 up to 10, grade 2+ whole class.
- **Naglieri Ability Index (NAI): mean 100, standard deviation 16, range 40-160.**

> **SD is 16, not 15.** Several prep sites say 15 while simultaneously stating
> that 68% of children score 84-116 — which is +/- 16 by definition. Pearson is
> authoritative.

| NAI | Percentile | Stanine |
|---|---|---|
| 135+ | 99 | 9 |
| 132 | 98 | 9 |
| 130 | 97 | 9 |
| 128 | 96 | 8 |
| 120 | 89 | 8 |
| 116 | 84 | 7 |
| 100 | 50 | 5 |

Above NAI 135 the percentile saturates at 99 and only the NAI discriminates.

**Precision.** Standard error of measurement is about **6 NAI points**, so a 95%
confidence interval is roughly **+/- 12 points**. A child scoring 130 has a true
score plausibly between 118 and 142. Alternate-form reliability averages r = .79
with a consistent **order effect of +3.8 NAI points on the second sitting** —
retesting inflates scores by about four points on average.

## The culture-fair claim, and its critics

Pearson: *"Because the NNAT3 items consist of geometric shapes that are universal
and have no verbal content ... NNAT3 has great utility ... especially for members
of groups that have been underrepresented."*

The equity evidence is genuinely contested and this app should not present it as
settled:

- Naglieri & Ford (2003) reported similar identification rates across groups.
- [Lohman (2005), *Gifted Child Quarterly*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001698620504900103) attacked the sampling: only 5.6% of the sample came from urban districts.
- [Lee et al. (2021) meta-analysis](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0016986221997800), 29 studies: NNAT correlates r = .68 with achievement, r = .31 with other intelligence measures, r = .20 with teacher ratings. It identifies proportionally more students of colour than other nonverbal tests but still falls short of its equity objective. Studies where Naglieri was an author showed notably higher correlations.

**Convergent validity with OLSAT-8** (NNAT3 manual, N = 366): NAI correlates
.55-.58 with OLSAT Total, .55-.66 with OLSAT Nonverbal, but only .25-.48 with
OLSAT Verbal. The two tests overlap but are not interchangeable — which is why
New York City used both.

Four structural facts that fall out of this table:

1. **There is a near-total break between Level C and Level D.** Only Arithmetic
   Reasoning and the four figural types cross it. A child moving from grade 2 to
   grade 3 meets an almost entirely different test.
2. **Arithmetic Reasoning is the only verbal type at all seven levels.**
3. **Picture Series exists at Level A and nowhere else.**
4. **Quantitative Reasoning is levels D-G only.**

LAUSD's published Level C map matches Pearson's Level C column exactly, nine for
nine, including the absence of Picture Series.

## Levels, items, timing

| Level | Grade | Verbal | Nonverbal | Total | Working time |
|---|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|---|
| A | K | 30 | 30 | 60 | ~77 min |
| **B** | **1** | 30 | 30 | **60** | ~77 min |
| **C** | **2** | 30 | 30 | **60** | ~72 min |
| **D** | **3** | 32 | 32 | **64** | 50-60 min |
| **E** | **4-5** | 36 | 36 | **72** | 60 min |

**Read-aloud rules.** Levels A and B are read entirely aloud, each item **once
only, no repeats**. Level C is read to students (LAUSD, which administers it to
about 30,000 second graders, states this plainly). From Level D students read
independently.

The read-aloud boundary is structural, not arbitrary: Following Directions and
Aural Reasoning are listening tasks that cannot exist in a student-read format,
and they exist at exactly levels A-C.

**Items are not ordered by difficulty.** OLSAT-8 uses a deliberate easy-hard-easy
spiral with item types **interleaved** throughout — the opposite of the NNAT's
monotonic ordering.

No penalty for guessing. Each section is preceded by unscored sample items.

## Scoring

`raw -> School Ability Index (SAI) -> percentile -> stanine / NCE`

- **SAI: mean 100, standard deviation 16, ceiling 150.**
- **The SAI exists only in the age-normed metric.** There is no grade-based SAI.
  Percentile, stanine and NCE are reported by both age and grade.
- Age norms are banded in three-month intervals, so two children with identical
  raw scores get different percentiles if they fall in different bands.
- Reported scales are **Verbal, Nonverbal and Total**. Clusters are reported as
  item-cluster summaries, not separate normed SAIs.

> SAI and NAI are both SD 16, so they compare to each other. **Neither compares
> to a Wechsler IQ (SD 15).** SAI 132 is +2.00 SD; WISC 132 is +2.13 SD.

## District cut scores

- **LAUSD**: universal Level C screening of all 2nd graders. Qualifying at age
  percentile 95+ on Total, **or** Verbal, **or** Nonverbal — any one scale
  qualifies independently. A 90-94 band applies for low-income meal codes.
  Percentile 99 on any scale triggers automatic referral.
- **Prince George's County MD**: grade 1 universal screening; percentile 81+
  triggers the next step.
- **New York City** (through 2021): OLSAT supplied the verbal half and NNAT2 the
  nonverbal half, 50/50. In 2012 NYC cut OLSAT's weight from two thirds to one
  third **because "the OLSAT is more preppable."**

## Design contract this app follows

1. **Two separate level-to-grade tables.** NNAT and OLSAT diverge from level D.
2. **NNAT: 5 options always. OLSAT: 5 typically, 4 at Level A.**
3. **NNAT palette is black, white, yellow, blue, green only**, and the answer is
   never carried by hue alone.
4. **Distractor design is the real substance.** For an item governed by *n* rules,
   write *n* distractors that each omit exactly one rule, plus one that changes
   an attribute the stem held invariant. Every documented item follows this.
5. **Read-aloud matters most for OLSAT levels A-C** and CogAT levels 5/6-8.
