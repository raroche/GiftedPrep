# Group Screening Tests for Gifted Identification — 20 Largest Florida School Districts

**Compiled:** August 2026
**Primary source:** Florida DOE ESE Policies and Procedures (SP&P) public repository, <https://beessgsw.org/#/spp/institution/public/> — specifically each district's **Part III** narrative and **Part V, Appendix C** ("District Plan to Increase the Participation of Underrepresented Students in the Program for Students Who Are Gifted"). Appendix C attachments are cited below as direct file URLs of the form `https://beessgsw.org/api/files/<id>`; each resolves to the district's own uploaded PDF/DOCX with its original filename.

Unless noted, SP&P citations are from the **2025-26 filing window** (FLDOE document `a2623ae8-024d-4b77-a4de-5aaf5575dc83`). Where a district's 2024-25 filing (`1db8a9b9-e792-498f-be88-f41b2d440596`) is identical, no separate citation is given.

### How to read this report

- **"NOT PUBLISHED"** means the fact is not stated in the district's state filing or on its public website. It does not mean the district lacks the practice — several districts clearly run a screener they decline to name publicly.
- Florida's two eligibility routes are **Plan A** (the statewide default under Rule 6A-6.03019, F.A.C. — IQ ≥ 2 SD above the mean, i.e. ~130) and **Plan B** (a district-specific, state-approved alternate plan for students who are limited English proficient or from low-socioeconomic-status families). Plan B is optional; a district must file and get approval for one.
- Anything marked **INFERENCE** is my reasoning, not a documented district statement.

## 1. Miami-Dade County Public Schools

- **Group screening instrument:** **NNAT3** (Naglieri Nonverbal Ability Test, 3rd Edition) **or NGAT-NV** (Naglieri General Ability Test–Nonverbal), district-administered. Source: [Appendix C – Student Evaluation Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/c23534cc-4f0e-4b3c-e8c5-08dc7b5442e8).
- **Universal screening grade:** **Grade 1.** The same document states the nonverbal test is "used to screen first grade students for possible gifted referral." Miami-Dade is the only district in this set that screens in grade 1 rather than grade 2.
- **Screening cut score:** Not stated as a single referral threshold. However, the **Gifted Eligibility Determination Form for Use with Underrepresented Students in First, Second, and Third Grade Utilizing District Administered Nonverbal Assessment** (form rev. 05-24, inside [Eligibility Criteria and Forms](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/7206cdee-5f72-4bf3-c804-08dc7fd21179)) scores Nonverbal Abilities as: **≥126 = 4 pts; 125–123 = 3; 122–120 = 2; 119–116 = 1; below 116 = 0.** A minimum of one point in the Nonverbal Abilities category is required, so **116 is the effective floor** for the Plan B route.
- **Talent-pool screening criteria (separate from the nonverbal test):** standardized test **89th percentile or higher**, FAST/EOC Level 4 or 5, or coursework grades of A or B, plus other indicators. All English Learner students earning A or B in ESOL/home-language arts and mathematics are automatically screened. Source: [Appendix C – Screening and Referral Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/c0ba0901-875f-4c68-e8c4-08dc7b5442e8).
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** WISC, Stanford-Binet, DAS, KABC, **RIAS**, UNIT, and **WNV** (Wechsler Nonverbal Scale of Ability). Ability Index scores from the district-administered NNAT3/NGAT-NV may themselves be used for the intellectual ability component. Source: [Appendix C – Student Evaluation Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/c23534cc-4f0e-4b3c-e8c5-08dc7b5442e8).
- **Plan B:** **Yes.** A student must obtain **a total of nine points across three of the four matrix categories, with at least one point in the Intellectual Ability category.** Separate forms exist for Kindergarten, grades 1–3, and grades 4–12. Source: [Eligibility Criteria and Forms](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/7206cdee-5f72-4bf3-c804-08dc7fd21179).
  - Full grades 1–3 matrix bands (same source): Gifted Characteristics Checklist 30–28 = 4, 27–24 = 3, 23–20 = 2, 19–16 = 1; Achievement percentile 99–95 = 4, 94–90 = 3, 89–85 = 2, 84–80 = 1; Nonverbal Abilities as above; Creativity Rating Scale ≥91 = 4, 90–86 = 3, 85–81 = 2, 80–77 = 1. Eligibility also requires a documented need for a special program and **16 or more checklist items rated 3 or 4**.
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** No separate individually-administered IQ floor is published; the matrix instead sets a **nonverbal screening floor of 116**.
- **Parent request and timeline:** A referral may be made by "a teacher, a parent/guardian, or any qualified individual who has knowledge of student's abilities"; a student profile including the Gifted Characteristics Checklist (FM 7051) goes to the **School Support Team (SST)**, which decides whether to continue. Source: [Appendix C – Screening and Referral Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/c0ba0901-875f-4c68-e8c4-08dc7b5442e8). Evaluation timeline in days: **NOT PUBLISHED** in the SP&P filing.
- **Test preparation / test security statement:** **NOT PUBLISHED.**

## 2. Broward County Public Schools

- **Group screening instrument:** For the referral pathway, the **Kaufman Brief Intelligence Test, 2nd Edition Revised (KBIT-2 Revised)**, "or other cognitive screening measure." For English Language Learners, the **Matrices** section of the KBIT-2 Revised or another nonverbal instrument (the plan names **NNAT**) may be used. Source: [Gifted_Plan B Revised May 2024](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/01999ef8-fe1e-45a2-1eb8-08dc861af6fb) (dated May 17, 2024) and [1 Screening and Referral Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/2592bdb9-801d-47e7-37c3-08dc856c37a6).
- **Universal screening grade:** **Grade 2.** "All second-grade students will be screened in the spring of each school year by the District using a uniform screening instrument. Students who meet specific criteria on the universal screening instrument will be automatically referred for consideration of eligibility." Separately, each fall all elementary schools review the prior year's data and screen students at the **80th percentile or above** in reading and/or mathematics, grades K-5. Same sources.
  - **Gap:** the state filing does **not name** the grade-2 universal instrument — it says only "a uniform screening instrument." The KBIT-2 Revised is named for the individual/referral pathway, not explicitly for the grade-2 mass administration.
- **Screening cut score:** **≥110** on the screening measure of intellectual functioning moves a student to evaluation. Explicitly, "If the student scores less than 110 on the screening measure of intellectual functioning, the student is no longer considered a candidate." A parent request for evaluation also triggers referral regardless of score. Source: [Gifted_Plan B Revised May 2024](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/01999ef8-fe1e-45a2-1eb8-08dc861af6fb).
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** DAS-II Normative Update School-Age, **WISC-V**, Leiter-3, CTONI-2, UNIT-2, **SB-5**, KABC-II NU, and **Raven's Progressive Matrices 2nd Edition (Raven's 2)**. Test selection depends on the student's age, language and cultural background, and psychologist judgment. Same source.
- **Plan B:** **Yes** — targeted at **ELL students** (codes LY, LN, LP, LF, LA) and **low-SES families** (free/reduced meals eligible and/or attending a Title 1 school). Eligibility uses the **Gifted Eligibility Matrix (GEM)**.
  - **Exact criteria:** "Gifted eligibility requires a **total score of 10 points on the GEM** and a student must score **at least one (1) point in the intellectual abilities category, the gifted characteristics category and the environmental indicators category.**"
  - **Gifted Characteristics:** select the 4 highest of 5 domains; **a minimum score of 40 earns 1 point**.
  - **Environmental Indicators:** primary language other than English (1 pt); identified Low SES (1 pt); meets criteria on the Underrepresented Student Traits Indicators Checklist (1 or 2 pts); additional ESE eligibility (1 pt).
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **112.** "The minimum standard score for consideration of eligibility on the GEM eligibility matrix is 112." Same source.
- **Parent request and timeline:** A student may be "referred by a parent, teacher or self-nominates themselves." If not referred for formal evaluation, parents get written notice in their native language where feasible plus a Notice of Refusal. Evaluation timeline in days: **NOT PUBLISHED** in the SP&P filing.
- **Test preparation / test security statement:** **NOT PUBLISHED.**

## 3. School District of Palm Beach County

- **Group screening instrument:** **NNAT3**, reported as the **Naglieri Ability Index (NAI)**. Source: [Gifted evaluation Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/790ad670-72b6-4464-e37a-08dca658cf16).
- **Universal screening grade:** **Grade 2** ("the Grade 2 screener", "NNAT3 Universal Screener"). Sources: same, plus [Gifted screening and referral updated](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/8da66f3a-ca00-4de1-abc1-08dcbaddfc52).
- **Screening cut score:** **NAI ≥ 120.** "Grade 2 students at or above the 120 Naglieri Ability Index (NAI) on the NNAT3 Universal Screener are being evaluated." Separately, for the Plan A route, "Students being considered for Plan A Gifted Eligibility must earn an overall score of **120 or higher** on a standardized IQ screening measure. This is different from Plan B as Plan A students must be screened prior to moving forward for evaluation." Sources: both files above.
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** The filing names the **WISC** family and the **WIAT-III** for achievement, and otherwise specifies "individually administered, standardized, and norm-referenced tests of intellectual functioning and academic achievement." A full instrument list is **NOT PUBLISHED**.
- **Plan B:** **Yes**, for students who are ELL or low socio-economic status (free/reduced lunch). Notably, **Plan B students bypass the screening gate** — they are referred by teacher and/or parent directly.
  - **Exact criteria:** "Students who are ELL or low socio-economic status (qualifying for free or reduced lunch) must have an **IQ test score ≥ 112** and receive a **total score of at least 20 points on the Gifted Plan B Matrix.**"
  - **Matrix components (5):** (1) IQ test on an individually administered instrument; (2) academic achievement on an individually administered achievement test in reading and mathematics; (3) classroom performance in Reading, Mathematics and Written Language, or Gifted Learner Performance K Skills for entering kindergarteners; (4) the Gifted Checklist for Underrepresented Populations; (5) student portfolio. Source: [Gifted screening and referral updated](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/8da66f3a-ca00-4de1-abc1-08dcbaddfc52).
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **112** (versus **130** for Plan A eligibility — "IQ test score ≥ 2 standard deviations (130 or above) on an individually administered IQ test").
- **Parent request and timeline:** Students "can be referred and screened by a teacher or parent as well as through the identification of the Grade 2 screener." The ESE Contact determines Plan A vs Plan B status, using the Confidentiality Agreement for Disclosure of Student Lunch Status Information (form PBSD 2528). **Timeline: 90 school days** — "The 90-day school day timeline is being followed for the testing of students." Source: [Gifted evaluation Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/790ad670-72b6-4464-e37a-08dca658cf16).
- **Test preparation / test security statement:** **NOT PUBLISHED.**

## 4. Hillsborough County Public Schools

- **Group screening instrument:** **None.** Hillsborough is a genuine outlier: its universal screening uses **existing data only**, not a group ability test. "School personnel will use existing data available through the District's electronic database for universal screening of all students at the elementary level for potential identification as gifted." Source: [Appendix C Screening and Referral](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/627eb73e-c0b8-4adc-7f39-08dc9059ae47).
- **Universal screening grade:** All elementary grades, via data review rather than a test administration. No single grade-level mass test administration is published.
- **Screening cut score:** **NOT PUBLISHED** — and by design. "Additional individual screening may be accomplished using multiple indicators and tools... **No single screening instrument should be used to categorically deny consideration for referral for a gifted evaluation.**" Additional individual screening may include a measure of intellectual development (individually administered), a measure of academic achievement, work samples, and checklists — none named. Same source.
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** **NOT PUBLISHED** by name; the filing refers generically to "a measure of intellectual development or ability (i.e., individually administered instrument)."
- **Plan B:** **Yes.** From [Eligibility Criteria](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/12971e21-5e17-401a-7f3a-08dc9059ae47) (a scanned PDF; text read from the page image), a student must:
  1. Be a member of a state-defined underrepresented group;
  2. Demonstrate intellectual ability **at or above one standard deviation above the mean** on an individually administered test of intellectual ability administered by a qualified evaluator;
  3. Demonstrate evidence of **two or more** of: (a) a score at the **75th percentile or higher** in reading or math on a State or other appropriate standardized achievement test; (b) a score of **36 or more of the 70** characteristics of giftedness on the **Potential Indicator Behavior Checklist (Form M)**; (c) a score of **10 or higher** on the student portfolio (Performance Example Rating Scale);
  4. Demonstrate a need for a special program for the gifted.
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **1 SD above the mean (~115).** Expressed as a standard deviation, not a fixed number.
- **Parent request and timeline:** "Additional screening may be initiated upon the request of a parent, a teacher, other school personnel, the student himself/herself, or a member of a community organization," expressed orally or in writing to the general education teacher, teacher of gifted, school counselor, or other school personnel. Parental consent is required before additional individual screening. Referrals go to the school's **Child Study Team (CST)**. Evaluation timeline in days: **NOT PUBLISHED** in the SP&P filing.
- **Test preparation / test security statement:** **NOT PUBLISHED.**

## 5. Orange County Public Schools

- **Group screening instruments:** Three named — **Otis-Lennon School Ability Test, 8th edition (OLSAT-8)**; **Naglieri Nonverbal Ability Test, 3rd edition (NNAT-3)**; and **Kaufman Brief Intelligence Test, 2nd edition (KBIT-2)**. Source: [Gifted Screening and Referral Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/c7f1dd87-5e40-469f-3a5b-08dcc2df5a3b).
- **Universal screening grade:** **Grade 2**, using the **NNAT-3**. "All students in the 2nd grade will be screened for gifted in the 2nd grade Universal Gifted Screening using the NNAT-3, unless they have already been staffed into the gifted program, have already been evaluated and found ineligible for gifted services, or had a cognitive evaluation that did not indicate gifted eligibility, as part of evaluation for another ESE program." The district also runs **two large group screenings per year** — fall and a larger spring administration. Source: [Gifted SPP](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/b5604ff4-b4ff-47ca-da0d-08dc8b9e5b9b).
- **Screening cut score:** **115 or higher.** "For underrepresented populations, a score of 115 or higher allows the student to be referred for the individual IQ test." Source: [Gifted Screening and Referral Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/c7f1dd87-5e40-469f-3a5b-08dcc2df5a3b).
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** **WISC-V**, **Stanford-Binet Fifth Edition (SB5)**, **Differential Abilities Scales (DAS-II)**, **Universal Nonverbal Intelligence Test**, and **Reynolds Intellectual Assessment Scale (RIAS)**. Bilingual psychologists are assigned to evaluate ELL students. Source: [Gifted Evaluation Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/6ae4307d-252a-4908-3a5c-08dcc2df5a3b).
- **Plan B:** **Yes** — the **Gifted Assessment Matrix**, for low-SES students and ELLs (LY, LF, LN, LP). This is the most granular published matrix of any district in this set. From [Gifted Eligibility Criteria](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/12f68593-a3f5-49c0-3a5d-08dcc2df5a3b):
  - **Two gates before the matrix is even completed:** (a) "Matrix is not completed for students who do not have a minimum score of **115** on a standardized test of intellectual development"; (b) "Matrix is not completed for students who do not have **two-thirds of the available points** on the appropriate checklist (**110** on the K-3 checklist, **120** on the 4-12 checklist)." At least one Need Justification must also be indicated.
  - **Intelligence Test points:** 115–116 = 5; 117–118 = 6; 119–120 = 7; 121–122 = 8; 123–124 = 9; 125+ = 10.
  - **Achievement Test percentile:** 96–99 = 5; 92–95 = 4; 88–91 = 3; 84–87 = 2; 80–83 = 1; <80 = 0.
  - **Gifted Characteristics Checklist (K-3):** 110–116 = 1; 117–122 = 2; 123–128 = 3; 129–134 = 4; 135–165 = 5. **(4-12):** 120–128 = 1; 129–136 = 2; 137–144 = 3; 145–152 = 4; 153–185 = 5.
  - **Academic Performance (12 pts max):** report card A = 3, B = 1, C or below = 0 in each of Reading/LA and Math; grade-level performance on grade level (or FSA Level 4 and below) = 0, +1 grade level (or FSA Level 5) = 2, +2 or more grade levels = 3.
  - **Student Portfolio (18 pts max):** Creativity, Leadership, Motivation scored 0–6 each.
  - **Minimum total for eligibility: 30 points for grades K-3; 35 points for grades 4-12.**
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **115.**
- **Parent request and timeline:** "Each school is responsible for reviewing student performance and records of all students," with input from "school personnel, parents, community members, and students." Students may be referred at other times for individual screenings. A web-based tutorial on the screening and referral process is provided to all instructional personnel. Evaluation timeline in days: **NOT PUBLISHED** in the SP&P filing.
- **Test preparation / test security statement:** **NOT PUBLISHED.**

## 6. Duval County Public Schools

- **Group screening instrument:** **Naglieri Non-Verbal Ability Test, 3rd Edition (NNAT3).** Source: [Underrepresented Gifted – Screening and Referral Procedures – Appendix C – July 2024](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/9186bf5a-0423-4b33-ae04-08dc9aa0cac0).
- **Universal screening grade:** **Grade 2.** "All district second graders will be screened using Naglieri Non-Verbal Ability Test 3rd Edition." Students in **K-1 and 3-12** who demonstrate gifted characteristics are nominated by a parent or teacher and screened with the same instrument. Same source.
- **Screening cut score:** **Standard score 107.** "Students will proceed to an individual intellectual assessment with a requisite standard score of **107** on the screener." This is the **lowest published screening cut** among the 20 districts. Same source.
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** **NOT PUBLISHED** by name. The filing refers to "a norm referenced standardized measure of ability" and permits **alternate composite scores** "in accordance with Department of Education Technical Assistance Paper FY 2005-9 and district written guidance on the appropriate use of alternate composite scores for intelligence measures." Source: [Underrepresented Gifted – Evaluation Procedures – Appendix C – July 2024](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/9eebee00-362a-45d4-ae05-08dc9aa0cac0).
- **Plan B:** **Yes.** Eligibility is determined by the **Eligibility Determination Team (EDT)** and requires:
  1. **115 Full Scale IQ** (*"SEM may be applied as appropriate"*; an alternate composite may be used), **AND**
  2. **20+ points on the Gifted Characteristics Checklist** — a checklist that "includes characteristics of giftedness that are commonly associated with underrepresented populations", **AND**
  3. Demonstrated need for differentiated instruction for the gifted.
  Source: [Underrepresented Gifted – Eligibility Criteria – Appendix C – July 2024](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/b32e11ab-95d4-452c-ae08-08dc9aa0cac0).
  There is **no point matrix** — it is a conjunctive three-part test.
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **115 FSIQ**, with standard error of measurement applicable.
- **Parent request and timeline:** Screening begins with "Teacher/Parent/Student Nomination — based on recognition of indicators of giftedness." Evaluation timeline in days: **NOT PUBLISHED** in the SP&P filing.
- **Test preparation / test security statement:** **NOT PUBLISHED.**

## 7. Pinellas County Schools

- **Group screening instrument:** **NNAT3.** Source: [Appendix C attachment Gifted 1](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/1889d6b3-787c-4f38-142d-08dc95dc0ade).
- **Universal screening grade:** **Grade 1 — but targeted, not district-wide.** The district's action plan states an intent to "Implement universal screening for gifted identification for **all first graders** in elementary schools" and "Implement universal screening for gifted identification for all students in **Transformation Zone** schools." The operational description is narrower: "**universal screening with the NNAT3 at schools with low gifted enrollment, high Limited English proficient population, and/or Title 1 schools** (low socio-economic status students)." Sources: [Appendix C attachment Gifted](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/335c0f55-ffcc-4f79-1ece-08dc861af6fb) and [Appendix C attachment Gifted 1](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/1889d6b3-787c-4f38-142d-08dc95dc0ade).
  - **Contradiction within the filing itself:** the goal statement says all first graders; the procedure section says only schools meeting the low-enrollment/high-LEP/Title 1 criteria. Treat district-wide grade 1 screening as aspirational rather than confirmed.
- **Screening cut score:** **NOT PUBLISHED.** The four-step process is documented ("Nomination – from parent or teacher; Screening using the Naglieri Nonverbal Ability Test - NNAT3; Referral; Evaluation") but no NNAT3 cut score appears anywhere in the filing.
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** **NOT PUBLISHED** by name — "An individually administered test of intelligence."
- **Plan B:** **Yes**, and it is **criteria-based with no point matrix.** To be identified under the plan, a student must be LEP or from a low-SES family per Rule 6A-6.03019, F.A.C., and demonstrate:
  1. "Intellectual ability by an intelligence quotient of **one standard deviation or more above the mean** on an individually administered standardized test of intelligence"; **AND**
  2. Evidence of **two or more** of: the majority of gifted-learner characteristics as measured by **a score of 3 or higher on the Gifted Characteristics Checklist**; academic achievement **at or above the 75th percentile** in reading or math on FAST (FST), STAR, or a Level 4 or 5 on the FSA/state assessment **within one calendar year** of the determination meeting; demonstration of creativity, motivation, leadership, or specific academic ability as measured by **a score of 4 or higher on a student portfolio rubric**.
  Source: [Gifted Eligibility Criteria](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/64d1b430-acc6-4200-1c11-08dc91247c3b) (scanned PDF, read from page image).
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **1 SD above the mean (~115).**
- **Parent request:** "**The parent or the teacher of the student can request that the student receive a gifted screening by submitting the request to the school psychologist.**" This is the most specific parent-request routing published by any district in this set. Source: [Appendix C attachment Gifted 1](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/1889d6b3-787c-4f38-142d-08dc95dc0ade). Evaluation timeline in days: **NOT PUBLISHED** in the SP&P filing.
- **Test preparation / test security statement:** **NOT PUBLISHED.**
- **Additional context:** 74 Pinellas elementary schools are designated Talent Development Schools where the teacher of gifted spends ~20% of instructional time with general education students, using the **Creativity Jot Down Sheet** (University of Western Kentucky) to surface referrals.

## 8. Polk County Public Schools

- **Group screening instruments:** **KBIT-2 Revised** (Revised Kaufman Brief Intelligence Test, Second Edition), **CogAT** (Cognitive Abilities Test), and **NNAT3**. For students coded LY, LF or LA for Limited English Proficiency, "the score from the matrices of the KBIT2 can be used as the instrument of measure." Sources: [Gifted part 1](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/f5201e0a-47c6-477b-b59b-08dc8489fa05) and [Gifted part 2](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/363cd105-8e59-4144-b59c-08dc8489fa05).
- **Universal screening grade:** **NOT PUBLISHED.** Polk's filing describes referral-driven screening only; no universal/mass administration grade is named.
- **Screening cut score:** **"around 114 or higher."** "To be considered for a formal evaluation, a student must achieve a score around 114 or higher on the Revised Kaufman Brief intelligence Test-Second Edition, (NNAT3), Cognitive Abilities Testing (CogAt)." The hedged wording ("around") is the district's own. Source: [Gifted part 2](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/363cd105-8e59-4144-b59c-08dc8489fa05).
- **Referral indicators:** Level 4 or 5 on FST, top 10% on benchmark tests, or 85% or higher on STAR. Low-SES defined as qualifying for SNAP, TANF, FIPR or Hearth. Source: [Gifted part 1](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/f5201e0a-47c6-477b-b59b-08dc8489fa05).
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** **NOT PUBLISHED** by name — "A school psychologist will administer an individual test of intelligence."
- **Plan B:** **Yes.** Requirements:
  - **Gifted Indicators Checklist:** "The student must earn at least **31 out of the 60 points** for gifted characteristics observed," rating leadership, motivation, creativity, and learning.
  - **Individual IQ:** "The minimum standard score for the consideration of eligibility on the intellectual assessment is **115** (one stanine above the norm)."
  - Confidence intervals may be used "at the discretion of the eligibility team." Partial scores may be used "when recommended in writing by the school psychologist."
  Source: [Gifted part 2](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/363cd105-8e59-4144-b59c-08dc8489fa05).
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **115.**
- **Parent request and timeline:** **NOT PUBLISHED** in the SP&P filing.
- **Test preparation / test security statement:** **NOT PUBLISHED.**

## 9. School District of Lee County

- **Group screening instrument:** **Naglieri General Ability Tests (NGAT)** — "The NGATs measure general ability in the areas of verbal, quantitative and nonverbal reasoning." Note this is the **NGAT**, the newer Naglieri battery, not the NNAT3. Source: [Gifted Referral Process 23-24](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/b987b533-9874-4d8d-1c01-08dc91247c3b).
- **Universal screening grade:** **Grade 2.** "All students in 2nd grade, with the exception of students who have qualified for gifted or to be on a modified curriculum, will be administered the Naglieri General Ability Tests (NGAT)." Same source.
- **Screening cut score:** **Standard Score 115 (84th percentile).** "All students who obtain a Standard Score of 115 (84th%ile) will be referred for a **full gifted evaluation**." Note Lee sends qualifying screener students straight to full evaluation. Same source.
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** **NOT PUBLISHED** by name. The filing discusses at length the use of **part scores versus full-scale scores** (a priori vs. a posteriori decisions) for students with hearing impairments or non-English speaking students. Source: [Appendix B SPP Gifted 2020](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/8874b595-cfbf-424a-bd14-08dc86f34136).
- **Plan B:** **Yes** — a **Matrix Scoring System for Under-Represented Groups**. From [Gifted Characteristics and Criteria](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/7134476f-a1ee-4303-e373-08dca658cf16):
  - **All three of the following must be met:** "Number of points scored in the **three highest categories must be 9**"; "**Minimum IQ 115**"; "**Minimum Rating Scale Score is 75**."
  - Matrix categories score 0–4 points each: rating scale, intelligence quotient, grades, portfolio score, and achievement test scores.
  - **Intelligence Quotient bands:** >124 = 4; 122–124 = 3; 119–121 = 2; 115–118 = 1; <115 = 0.
  - **Rating Scale bands:** 126–140 = 4; 105–125 = 3; 85–104 = 2; 75–84 = 1; <75 = 0.
  - **Achievement K-2 (reading or math percentile):** >90 = 4; 80–89 = 3; 70–79 = 2; 60–69 = 1; <60 = 0. **Grades 3-12** use FSA levels; **Grades:** 94–100 = 4; 85–93 = 3; 75–84 = 2; 65–74 = 1; <65 = 0. **Portfolio:** Very Strong / Strong / Moderate / Fair / Weak.
  - The **Gifted Characteristics Checklist** (separate grade-banded forms for K-2, 3-5, 6-12) defines "majority of characteristics" as **at least 75 points**.
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **115.**
- **Parent request and timeline:** "A student is brought forward for consideration of gifted eligibility through a universal screening checklist, teacher, parent, peer, or self nomination." The teacher completes the Gifted Nomination Form and screening checklist; the **Gifted Assessment Team (GAT)** — at minimum a school-based administrator and the classroom teacher, teacher of the gifted, and/or school counselor — reviews grades, FSA/STAR/iReady scores.   - **Contradiction flagged.** Both documents are attached to the same current SP&P filing. **INFERENCE:** the 2024-era "Gifted Appendix Revision" supersedes the July 2020 document, making **90th percentile** the operative figure and the 74th-percentile number stale. This is an inference from document recency, not a district statement — Alachua has not withdrawn the older attachment.
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** **RIAS-2** (Reynolds Intellectual Assessment Scales), **WISC-V**, or **NNAT3** (Naglieri Nonverbal Ability Test, 3rd Edition) used in individual administration. Source: [Plan B Student Evaluation Procedures](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/04ddfbb5-f717-4d70-7c44-08dc9772c7e0).
- **Plan B:** **Yes** — the **Gifted Eligibility Determination Form Plan B (Matrix)**, incorporating measures of intellectual development, academic performance, leadership, motivation, and creativity. "Gifted eligibility requires a **total score of 10 points or higher** and the student must **meet the minimum eligibility requirement in each of the four categories** on the Plan B Matrix." All Plan B students must have reading and math achievement scores reportable in percentiles or standard scores. Source: Alachua SP&P Part V, Appendix C.
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **NOT PUBLISHED** as a specific number; expressed only through the matrix's per-category minimums, which are not published in the filing text.
- **Parent request and timeline:** "**A teacher, parent/guardian, or community member may make a request for an initial student evaluation to the school-based team.**" The team considers the Pre-referral Checklist of Gifted Characteristics, standardized achievement performance, and the group or individual screening abilities test. **Waiver provision:** "A **minimum of three professional educators** at the school where the student is attending may request a **waiver of the prereferral requirements** for students who meet criteria for a referral for IQ testing based on the Gifted Checklist but fall short of the criteria for achievement testing or the criteria for the ability screening test if professional educators (**including a school psychologist**) believe that the achievement test or the ability screening test is not a good indicator of the student's current academic performance." Sources: Alachua SP&P Part III narrative and [Gifted Appendix Revision](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/7c63b4ea-11b1-4e5c-a06c-08dca5a6fab6). Evaluation timeline in days: **NOT PUBLISHED** in the SP&P filing.
- **Test preparation / test security statement:** **NOT PUBLISHED.** The nearest equivalent is the requirement that ability/achievement scores be "given within one year of referral."

## 19. Leon County Schools

- **Group screening instrument:** **KBIT-2** (Kaufman Brief Intelligence Test-2). Source: [Screening and Referral Process](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/f0fd714a-6abc-47c7-a948-08dc8ebd8ea2) (scanned PDF, read from page image).
- **Universal screening grade:** **None published.** Leon's process is referral-driven with a **behavioral gate before any testing**: "For students suspected of being gifted, teachers will complete a **Behavioral Characteristics of Gifted Students Checklist prior to any testing**. Students who achieve the requisite score of **at least 36** on the Behavioral Characteristics Checklist are then administered the Kaufman Brief Intelligence Test-2 (KBIT-2)." Same source.
  - The district also runs a safety net: "the ESE office reviews **all Plan A ineligible students' information** to ensure teams do not overlook the Plan B option for students who meet the criteria."
- **Screening cut score:** **NOT PUBLISHED.** No KBIT-2 cut score appears in the filing; the published gate (36 points) is on the behavioral checklist, not the ability test. This information is captured on the **Gifted Screening Summary**.
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** **NOT PUBLISHED** by name.
- **Plan B:** **Yes** — the **Gifted Matrix Summary (Plan B)**, dated **05/2024**, for English Language Learner or low-socio-economic-status students. The form also requires confirming "Student has been given the opportunity to meet Gifted Plan A screening criteria." From [Eligibility Criteria](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/ff80e33d-41e1-457e-a97a-08dc8ebd8ea2) (scanned PDF, read from page image):
  - **I. Behavioral Characteristics of Gifted Students:** 55–72 = 2 pts; 36–54 = 1 pt (no 3- or 4-point bands exist).
  - **II. Gifted Rating Scales Creativity Assessment (T-score):** ages 5–9 — 75+ = 4, 70–74 = 3, 65–69 = 2, 60–64 = 1; ages 10–13 — 73+ = 4, 69–72 = 3, 65–68 = 2, 60–64 = 1.
  - **III. Academic Achievement (Total Battery percentile, or individual test percentile):** above 95 = 4; 90–95 = 3; 85–89 = 2; 80–84 = 1.
  - **Intermediate gate:** "**If subtotal is 6 points or above in components I, II, and III, proceed to component IV.**"
  - **IV. Intellectual Ability (Full Scale/Composite):** 125–129 = 4; 121–124 = 3; 118–120 = 2; **115–117 = 1**.
  - **Total: "10 points are required for gifted placement. (At least one point must be earned in Component IV Intellectual Ability.)"**
  - **V.** A statement of need from the current classroom teacher.
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **115.**
- **Parent request and timeline:** "For students suspected of being gifted, parents will be **referred to the home-zone referral coordinator** so they may assist the parent in the referral process." For children not enrolled in any school, "they are referred by the LCS ESE Office to their home-zone school referral coordinator." Source: Leon SP&P Part III narrative. **Timeline: NOT PUBLISHED as a number** — the filing says only that "Students suspected of being gifted will be evaluated within a reasonable [time]," in contrast to the explicit 90-day commitments made by Brevard, Pasco, Osceola and Palm Beach.

## 20. Marion County Public Schools

- **Group screening instrument:** **None — Marion screens with a teacher rating scale, not a group ability test.** The primary screener is the **Gifted Rating Scales (GRS)**; the secondary is the **Gifted and Talented Checklist**. Sources: [Gifted - Screening and Referral Process](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/b5ec0c77-a799-4518-ccad-08dc9443b4fc) and [Gifted - Evaluation Process](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/e0f7cd76-a282-4b31-ccae-08dc9443b4fc).
  - **GRS qualifying criteria:** "**T scores of 60 or higher on 3 out of 6 sections (1st-8th grade) and 3 out of 5 scales (KG only) are required; one of the qualifying scales must be the Intellectual Ability Scale.**"
  - "As the GRS protocol is only valid for students aged **6 through 13**, the requirement for the GRS to be completed will be **waived for referred 5 year olds**."
  - "**More than one teacher is recommended** to participate and collaborate in the screener for each student," with teachers in the student's area(s) of strength — including special area teachers and media specialists — highly recommended.
- **Universal screening grade:** **NOT PUBLISHED** — referral-driven.
- **Screening cut score:** The GRS threshold above (T ≥ 60 on 3 of 6 scales, one being Intellectual Ability) functions as the screening gate. There is **no group ability-test cut score** because there is no group ability test. If the screener does not show a majority of characteristics (at least 2/3) and performance data does not demonstrate need, "the referral is stopped and parents are notified (School Counselor)."
- **Individually administered IQ tests:** The most complete published list of any district — **C-TONI-2, DAS-II, KABC-II, NNAT-3 (Individual Administration), Raven's Progressive Matrices 2nd Edition (Raven's 2), RIAS-2, SB5, Early SB-5, TONI-4, UNIT2, WPPSI-IV, WISC-V, and WISC-IV Spanish.** "The MCPS Office of Psychological Services will select the appropriate intellectual evaluations for the student." Source: [Gifted - Evaluation Process](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/e0f7cd76-a282-4b31-ccae-08dc9443b4fc).
- **Plan B:** **Yes** — the **Gifted Matrix for Plan B Eligibility** (form eESE161). From [Gifted - Eligibility Criteria](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/07bfe14e-e4f6-4dfe-37ea-08dc856c37a6) and the Evaluation Process document:
  - "Students from underrepresented groups with a score at least **one standard deviation above average (minimum IQ score of 116)** on an individually administered IQ instrument may use the Gifted Identification Matrix."
  - "Students must earn a **minimum of 75% on the Gifted Matrix**, have a written need for a special program, and have a **majority of gifted characteristics** (at least 2/3) on the Gifted Screener or the Gifted and Talented Checklist."
  - "**Eligibility requires a total score of fifteen (15) or higher and a minimum rating of one (1) in each category.**"
  - **Matrix categories:** (1) Intellectual; (2) Checklist — 63–71 = 4, 55–62 = 3, 45–54 = 2, 37–44 = 1; (3) Academic Performance — FSA Level 5 = 4, Level 4 = 3; national norm assessment percentile 95–99 = 4, 90–94 = 3, 85–89 = 2, 80–84 = 1; iReady; grade-level acceleration (three/two/one grade levels ahead); most recent quarter's report card A's and B's in 100%/85%/70%/55% of subjects; (4) Product/Portfolio with an attached rubric, three products/performances scored.
  - **"Confidence Interval cannot be used for Plan B"** — though it may be used for Plan A. This is an unusual asymmetry and works against underrepresented students.
  - All forms are available in **English and Spanish**.
- **Plan B IQ threshold:** **116** — a distinctive figure. Most peer districts use 115; Marion's is one point higher and admits no confidence interval.
- **Parent request and timeline:** Referral begins with a nomination; the **school counselor** obtains Permission to Screen, emails the GRS to the teacher, receives it back, and if a majority of characteristics is demonstrated, obtains Consent for Evaluation with Procedural Safeguards. "Student's referral packet is submitted by counselor to Psychological Services with information of student's underrepresented status." A **parent nomination form** is part of the Plan A need documentation. Source: [Gifted - Evaluation Process](https://beessgsw.org/api/files/e0f7cd76-a282-4b31-ccae-08dc9443b4fc). Evaluation timeline in days: **NOT PUBLISHED** in the SP&P filing.
- **Test preparation / test security statement:** **NOT PUBLISHED.**

## Cross-cutting observations

**The NNAT3 is the dominant instrument.** Nine of the twenty districts use a Naglieri instrument as their primary group screener (Miami-Dade, Palm Beach, Orange, Duval, Pinellas, Polk, Lee, Volusia, Osceola), and Alachua and Marion use NNAT-3 in individual administration. Lee has moved to the newer **NGAT**, and Miami-Dade names the **NGAT-NV** alongside the NNAT3 — an indication that the Naglieri General Ability Test is beginning to replace the NNAT3 in Florida.

**Grade 2 is the near-universal screening year.** Eleven districts screen in grade 2. Miami-Dade (grade 1) and Pinellas (grade 1, targeted) are the exceptions among districts that screen at all.

**Screening cut scores vary by a full standard deviation** — from Duval's 107 to Manatee's 122. A child who would be referred for evaluation in Duval or Broward would be turned away in Manatee, St. Johns (Plan A), or Palm Beach. Since these are all standard scores on comparable ability measures, this is a genuine and large difference in access, not an artifact of different scales.

**Five districts publish no group screener at all.** Hillsborough screens from existing data by design; Marion screens with a teacher rating scale; Manatee uses individually administered brief IQ screeners; Sarasota screens only on referral; and Pasco publishes nothing.

**Plan B is not universal.** Pasco explicitly declines to operate one, meaning Pasco families have no reduced-IQ pathway. Seminole files a plan but deliberately refuses to reduce it to a matrix or cut score.

**Plan B IQ thresholds cluster tightly at 115** (Orange, Duval, Polk, Lee, Brevard, Volusia, Osceola, St. Johns, Leon, plus Hillsborough and Pinellas expressed as 1 SD). The outliers matter: Broward and Palm Beach set **112**, the most generous; Marion sets **116** and forbids confidence intervals; Sarasota requires **120** unless paired with 90th-percentile achievement; and Manatee requires **1.5 SD (~122)**, by far the most restrictive.

**Procedural fine print can decide cases.** Duval permits the standard error of measurement; Brevard explicitly forbids it. Marion allows a confidence interval for Plan A but bars it for Plan B. Polk leaves confidence intervals to team discretion. For a child scoring 113–114, these clauses determine eligibility.

**Only four districts publish test-security rules**, and they are the most practically important findings for parents:
- **St. Johns** — CogAT and KBIT-2R may each be taken **only once in a K-12 career**.
- **Sarasota** — same instrument not within 12 months; more than three screenings across a school career discouraged.
- **Manatee** — screening with RIST bars the RIAS for eligibility; screening with WASI-II bars all Wechsler scales; most students evaluated once.
- **Osceola** — referral for evaluation at most once per year.

**No district in this set publishes any statement about test preparation.** None endorses, discourages, or prohibits commercial prep. The test-security rules above are the only published constraints, and they operate by limiting attempts rather than by addressing preparation.

## Method and caveats

Findings were drawn from each district's own SP&P filing in the FLDOE repository, retrieved through the site's public JSON API: the institution list at `/api/institutions`, the SP&P document list at `/api/Spp/GetDocuments`, each district's responses at `/api/Spp/documents/{documentId}/Institutions/{institutionId}`, and each Appendix C attachment at `/api/files/{fileId}`. Several districts' attachments are scanned images with no text layer (Hillsborough, Leon, Osceola, Pinellas, Volusia, Alachua); those were rendered to images and read visually, and are marked as such in the relevant sections.

The SP&P filing is the authoritative legal instrument — it is what the district commits to the state and what the state approves. Where a district website differs, the filing generally governs, but websites are often more current on operational details such as testing windows and contact routing.

Two limitations to note. First, "NOT PUBLISHED" is a statement about the public record, not about district practice; several districts plainly administer instruments and thresholds they do not disclose (Broward's grade-2 universal instrument is the clearest example). Second, cut scores and instruments change between filing cycles; every figure here should be re-verified against the current year's filing before being relied on for an individual child.

Commercial test-preparation websites were excluded as sources entirely, per the research brief; they are a frequent source of stale and incorrect Florida district criteria.
